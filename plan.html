Half Ironman · Winter Run Block

Mastering the Run
5 October 2026 – 3 January 2027

Thirteen weeks with one job: build a runner's legs slowly enough that they never break. Four runs a week, growing by about five minutes at a time. Cycling drops to roughly one Sunday a month — your pick, when the forecast looks kind. Swimming ticks over twice a week and asks nothing of you. Target: IRONMAN 70.3 Bolton, Sunday 6 June 2027.

The governing rule of this block. Tight calves and a long lay-off from running are the two biggest predictors of injury in a returning runner, and you have both. So: every run starts with Routine D, ends with Routine A, and the long run grows by roughly five minutes a week — around half the rate most plans use. If you feel good, that is not a reason to add more. It is the plan working.

The long run, week by week

Minutes, not miles — time on feet is what builds durability, and it stops you chasing pace on tired legs.

Three weeks up, one week down. The dips at weeks 16, 20 and 24 are where the adaptation actually happens.

Why Monday and Thursday have no run. Four runs spread Tue / Wed / Fri / Sun gives you two full non-impact days a week, and puts the two swim days exactly where the legs need the break. The Wednesday run is deliberately the shortest and easiest of the week — it exists to add frequency, not load.

The monthly ride

One Sunday a month, swap the long run for a ride instead.

You choose which Sunday — watch the forecast and take the good one. The principle is simple: run in the rain, ride in the sun. A wet long run costs you nothing but comfort; a wet winter ride on cold roads is genuinely miserable and slightly dangerous.

The one rule when you swap: repeat that long-run duration the following week rather than jumping two steps up the ladder. So if you ride instead of the 80-minute run in week 18, week 19 becomes 80 minutes rather than 85. You lose a week of progression, which is exactly the right price.

Cheapest weeks to swap — weeks 14, 18 and 22 sit in the middle of their blocks, so losing one long run there costs least. Avoid swapping week 23: that 100-minute run is the milestone the whole block is built towards.
Being straight about the trade. Riding once a month means your bike fitness will slip over the winter. That is a real cost, and it is still the right call — cycling fitness returns quickly, running durability does not, and yours is the thing that needs twelve unbroken weeks. The Wednesday commute ride comes back in March when the mornings get light, and the spring block rebuilds the bike properly with plenty of time before June. If you want to soften the dip, a 30–45 minute spin on any evening you feel fresh is welcome, but it is entirely optional and never at the cost of sleep.

The five routines

D and A bracket every single run. E is new and is the most important addition in this block for tight calves.

Routine D — pre-run dynamic

5 min · before EVERY run
  1. Ankle pogos — x20
  2. Leg swings front–back — x10/side
  3. Leg swings side–side — x10/side
  4. Walking lunges — x8/side
  5. High knees 20 m, heel flicks 20 m
  6. 2 × 20s strides, building to brisk

Never static stretch cold muscles. This replaces it.

Routine A — post-run stretch

10 min · after EVERY run
  1. Calf, straight leg (wall) — 45–60s/side
  2. Calf, bent knee — soleus; the one that bites returning runners
  3. Standing quad
  4. Hamstring, heel on step
  5. Kneeling hip-flexor lunge — squeeze the rear glute
  6. Figure-4 glute

Routine E — calf, Achilles & hip

12 min · Mon + Thu, after weights
  1. Eccentric heel drops, straight leg — 3×12/side, lower over 3 seconds
  2. Eccentric heel drops, bent knee — 3×12/side
  3. Single-leg calf raises — 2×15/side
  4. Tibialis raises, back to wall — 2×20; protects shins
  5. Single-leg glute bridge — 2×12/side
  6. Clamshells / side-lying leg raises — 2×15/side
  7. Copenhagen plank or adductor squeeze — 2×20s/side

Strong calves beat stretched calves. This is the block's insurance policy.

Routine B — full leg & back

20 min · Wed + Sun evenings
  1. All of Routine A at 60s holds
  2. Adductor rock-backs — x8–10/side
  3. Lying spinal twist — 60s/side
  4. Pigeon — 60–90s/side
  5. Downward dog, pedalling the heels — 60s
  6. Ankle circles + kneeling shin stretch
  7. Doorway chest + overhead lat reach
  8. Child's pose — 60s

Routine C — foam rolling

20 min · Saturday evenings
  1. Calves — 90s/side
  2. Hamstrings — 60s/side
  3. Quads — 90s/side
  4. Outer thigh / TFL — roll above the ITB, gently
  5. Glutes — 60s/side, ankle over knee
  6. Upper back — never the lower back
  7. Finish with calf + hip-flexor holds

Six rules for staying healthy through this block

1. Easy means easy. If you cannot hold a conversation, you are running too fast. Roughly nine of every ten minutes this winter should be genuinely comfortable.
2. Never add a bonus run. The plan already grows as fast as tendons adapt. Feeling good is not a reason to extend.
3. Cut Friday first. If something feels tight, drop the Friday run and keep Sunday. If it still feels tight on Sunday, drop that too and lose nothing.
4. Any niggle that changes your gait means stop. Limping through a run converts a week off into a month off.
5. Missed sessions are gone. Never cram two into one day, and never move a long run to sit next to another run.
6. Routine E is not optional. Twice a week, twelve minutes. Eccentric calf loading is the best-evidenced injury prevention there is for exactly your problem.
